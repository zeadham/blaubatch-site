Standard PP copolymer carriers offer impact resistance and flexibility. Homopolymer carriers (PPH) deliver higher stiffness and better heat deflection. The FMPP-2180 exists because woven sack and strapping producers need that rigidity — a copolymer carrier would introduce unwanted flexibility into the final product.

#### Applications

Injection molding, thermoforming, woven sacks, raffia, strapping, furniture components, automotive interior parts, industrial containers, and construction profiles. If it is PP-based and volume-driven, there is an FMPP grade for it.

# 5. INDUSTRY INSIGHT — FILLER MASTERBATCH IN PACKAGING & COST OPTIMIZATION

## Blog Post

### Headline Options
1. **How Filler Masterbatch Reduces Packaging Costs Without Reducing Quality**
2. **CaCO₃ Filler Masterbatch in Packaging: The Economics of Smart Formulation**
3. **Cost Optimization in Plastic Packaging: Where Filler Masterbatch Fits**

### Body

Resin is the single largest cost in plastic packaging production. When virgin polymer prices fluctuate — and they always do — converters need levers they can pull without redesigning their products or requalifying their processes.

Filler masterbatch is one of those levers. And when it is formulated correctly, it is the most effective one.

#### The Economics Are Simple

CaCO₃ (calcium carbonate) costs a fraction of virgin polyethylene or polypropylene resin. A filler masterbatch loaded at 70–80% CaCO₃ on a compatible carrier resin allows you to displace a significant portion of virgin polymer in your formulation — directly reducing material cost per kilogram of finished product.

At 20–30% let-down ratios (the percentage of filler masterbatch in your total blend), converters typically see material cost reductions of 10–25%, depending on the application and resin prices.

#### But Cost Is Only Half the Equation

Poorly formulated filler masterbatch creates more problems than it solves. Inconsistent particle size distribution leads to die buildup. Wrong carrier compatibility causes poor dispersion. Batch-to-batch variation forces constant process adjustment.

The result? Downtime, scrap, and customer complaints that cost more than the filler saved.

This is why carrier selection matters. This is why CaCO₃ sourcing matters. This is why manufacturing consistency is not a marketing claim — it is the difference between a cost-saving strategy and an expensive mistake.

#### Where Filler Masterbatch Works Best in Packaging

- **Blown film** — Shopping bags, garbage bags, industrial liners. LLDPE carrier filler maintains tear resistance while reducing cost.
- **Cast film and sheet** — Thermoformed trays, lids, protective packaging. Controlled loading improves stiffness — a benefit in rigid packaging.
- **Woven sacks and raffia** — Agricultural bags, cement bags, bulk packaging. PPH carrier filler adds stiffness and reduces raw material cost at high volume.
- **Injection-molded packaging** — Containers, closures, pails. PP carrier filler improves cycle time by enhancing heat transfer and dimensional stability.

#### What Filler Masterbatch Does Not Do

It does not make bad products acceptable. It does not replace engineering. And it does not work if your supplier treats every batch differently.

Filler masterbatch is a precision input. Treat it like one.

# 7. TECHNICAL/EDUCATIONAL — CaCO₃ LOADING & CARRIER SYSTEMS

## Blog Post

### Headline Options
1. **CaCO₃ Loading in Filler Masterbatch: What 70%, 75%, and 80% Actually Mean for Your Process**
2. **Carrier Resin Selection in Filler Masterbatch — LLDPE, LDPE, PP, and PPH Explained**
3. **A Technical Guide to Filler Masterbatch: Loading Ratios, Carrier Systems, and Process Impact**

### Body

Not all filler masterbatch is created equal. Two products can both claim "75% CaCO₃" and behave completely differently on your line. The difference comes down to two things: the calcium carbonate itself and the carrier resin it is compounded with.

#### What CaCO₃ Loading Means

Loading percentage refers to the weight fraction of calcium carbonate in the masterbatch pellet. A 75% loaded product contains 75% CaCO₃ and 25% carrier resin (plus minor processing additives).

Higher loading = more mineral content per kilogram of masterbatch = more resin displacement at a given let-down ratio.

Here is what that looks like in practice:

| Masterbatch Loading | Let-Down Ratio | CaCO₃ in Final Product |
|---|---|---|
| 70% | 20% | 14% |
| 75% | 20% | 15% |
| 80% | 20% | 16% |
| 70% | 30% | 21% |
| 80% | 30% | 24% |

The difference between 70% and 80% loading at a 30% let-down is 3 percentage points of CaCO₃ in your final product. That is a meaningful cost difference at production scale.

#### But Higher Is Not Always Better

At 80% loading, you are pushing the limits of dispersion. The carrier resin has less room to encapsulate and distribute the mineral particles. If the CaCO₃ particle size distribution is not tightly controlled, or if the compounding process is not precise, you get:

- **Die buildup** — agglomerates accumulating at the die lip
- **Surface defects** — fish eyes, gels, rough texture
- **Reduced mechanical properties** — brittleness, lower elongation, tear failure
- **Inconsistent output** — thickness variation, weight fluctuation

The right loading for your application depends on what your process can tolerate and what your end product requires.

#### Carrier Resin: The Other Half of the Equation

The carrier resin is not packaging for the CaCO₃ — it becomes part of your formulation. It must be compatible with your base resin to disperse properly and not introduce processing issues.

**LLDPE Carrier**
Best for: blown film, stretch film, bags, liners
Why: maintains elongation and tear resistance at high filler loads. Blends naturally with LLDPE and HDPE base resins.

**LDPE Carrier**
Best for: LDPE-dominant blends, coating, lamination
Why: lower melt viscosity improves flow in LDPE systems. Better for processes where the base resin is already LDPE.

**PP (Copolymer) Carrier**
Best for: injection molding, thermoforming, general PP extrusion
Why: impact-modified PP maintains toughness. Compatible with random and block copolymer base resins.

**PPH (Homopolymer) Carrier**
Best for: woven sacks, raffia, strapping, high-stiffness applications
Why: higher crystallinity = higher stiffness and heat deflection temperature. Critical for applications where rigidity is the specification.

#### Mismatch Is Expensive

Using a PP-carrier filler in a PE process — or an LDPE carrier when your base is LLDPE — introduces compatibility issues that show up as poor dispersion, gel formation, or mechanical failure. These problems are not always visible immediately. They show up in QC testing, customer complaints, or worse — in the field.

Match the carrier to your base resin. Match the loading to your application requirements. Then find a supplier who delivers the same product every time.
