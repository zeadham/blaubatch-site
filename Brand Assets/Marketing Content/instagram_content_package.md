# 7. TECHNICAL EDUCATION — CaCO₃ Loading & Carrier Systems

### Visual Suggestion
Carousel post (7 slides) — educational/save-worthy:
1. Title card: "FILLER MASTERBATCH 101" — "Loading % & Carrier Resin Explained"
2. "What loading means" — 70% vs 75% vs 80% visual breakdown (pellet cross-section graphic)
3. Loading comparison table: loading × let-down = CaCO₃ in final product
4. "Higher is not always better" — die buildup, surface defects, brittleness (warning icons)
5. Carrier resin guide: LLDPE → film | LDPE → LDPE blends | PP → injection | PPH → woven sacks
6. "Mismatch is expensive" — wrong carrier = poor dispersion, gels, mechanical failure
7. "Match the grade to the process" — CTA: "Not sure? We will help → link in bio"

### Caption

"75% CaCO₃" on a spec sheet means nothing without context.

Two filler masterbatch products can have identical loading and behave completely differently on your line.

What loading percentage actually means:
The weight fraction of calcium carbonate in the pellet. 75% loaded = 75% CaCO₃ + 25% carrier resin.

Higher loading = more resin displacement = more cost savings.
But also = harder to disperse = more risk of defects.

At 80% loading with poor particle size control:
→ Die buildup
→ Fish eyes and surface defects
→ Brittleness and tear failure
→ Inconsistent film thickness

The carrier resin matters just as much:

LLDPE carrier → blown film, bags, liners
Maintains elongation and tear resistance at high loads.

LDPE carrier → LDPE-dominant blends, coating
Lower melt viscosity, better flow in LDPE systems.

PP copolymer → injection molding, thermoforming
Impact resistance and toughness.

PPH homopolymer → woven sacks, strapping, raffia
Maximum stiffness and heat deflection.

Using a PP carrier in a PE process? That is not a shortcut — it is a recall waiting to happen.

Match the carrier to your resin.
Match the loading to your application.
Then find a supplier who delivers the same product every time.

- **Educational posts** (5, 7): Save-worthy, shareable technical content — builds authority

## Posting Recommendations
- **Format:** Carousels outperform single images for B2B on Instagram — use them for 5 of the 7 posts
- **Frequency:** 2–3 posts per week
- **Best times for B2B:** Tuesday–Thursday, 9–11 AM (target audience timezone)
- **Stories:** Repurpose each post as a 3–4 frame story with swipe-up (or "link in bio") CTA
- **Reels:** Consider 15–30 second reels for posts 4 (partnership) and 6 (company story) — factory footage, product close-ups, team shots
